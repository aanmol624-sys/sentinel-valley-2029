Team

Course topics on this page: 18. Project HR management 19. Motivating people 20. Resource planning

A good team needs more than a good budget (Renso, 2026). Sentinel Valley needs about 300 people at its peak, many with skills the valley does not have yet.

The wage benchmark for technical roles is the B.C. median for data scientists, $48.08 per hour (Job Bank, 2025), or about $100,000 a year. I add 25% for benefits, giving $125,000 per year in the budget. A shortage of qualified people is a top reason organizations outsource today (Renso, 2026). Where B.C. lacks skills, we bring in contractors in the short term while UFV trains locals for the long term.

19How I will motivate people

Intrinsic motivation comes from enjoying the work, and extrinsic motivation comes from rewards or penalties (Renso, 2026). This project has a rare intrinsic advantage: people get to protect their own families and neighbours. I will use that, along with the methods below.

  • Following Maslow, salaries and job security cover the basic needs, and the team meets the people it protects. Building a new regional digital twin gives people the kind of creative work Maslow places at the top (Renso, 2026).
  • Herzberg calls salary, benefits and good equipment hygiene factors: they prevent dissatisfaction but do not motivate. The real motivators are achievement, recognition, responsibility and growth (Renso, 2026). Milestones will be celebrated publicly, and pillar leads will have real authority. Each staff member also gets a training budget.
  • I want to lead mainly through expert and referent power: knowledge and trust. Thamhain and Wilemon found "work challenge" and "expertise" to be strong influence bases (Renso, 2026).
  • The five team dysfunctions are absence of trust, fear of conflict, lack of commitment, avoidance of accountability and inattention to results (Renso, 2026). I reduce them by keeping working teams at 3–7 people, holding short regular meetings, never blaming in public, and putting results on a shared dashboard.

20Resource planning

Work is assigned through the steps from the lectures: finalize requirements, decide how work will be done, break down the work, then assign it through an organizational breakdown structure (Renso, 2026). The biggest danger is resource overloading. Developers are needed heavily in 2028 and only for tweaks later, while HVAC crews are needed only in summer. I level resources: temporary contractors cover the 2028 development peak, and HVAC crews work on care homes and farm housing between the two school summers.

To assign work, I use a responsibility assignment matrix, so every WBS item has exactly one person accountable for it. The real constraint is scarce skills: certified air-balancing technicians and genomic analysts are hard to hire in B.C. I book them early through procurement contracts and the BCCDC training partnership, and the PMO keeps a quarterly resource chart so that an overload shows up months before it happens, not on the day work is late.

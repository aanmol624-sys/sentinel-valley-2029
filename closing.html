Closing the project by December 31, 2029

Course topics on this page: 25. Closing process

The project continues for three months after go-live. Closing formalizes acceptance of the project (Renso, 2026).

25The closing process

  1. Final scope validation (Oct 2029): each of the 12 deliverables is signed off by its acceptor using the checklist written in planning.
  2. Close procurements (Oct–Nov 2029): confirm vendors delivered, release holdbacks, settle claims, archive contracts.
  3. Handover to operations (Nov 2029): the network moves from the project team to a permanent "Sentinel Valley Operations Unit" at UFV, with staff, manuals and the $68.6M operating reserve for 2030–2034.
  4. Lessons learned (Dec 2029): answer the four questions from class. Did we meet scope, time and cost? What went right? What went wrong? What will we do differently next time? (Renso, 2026)
  5. Release resources and celebrate (Dec 2029): team members get performance appraisals and references, and the project is celebrated with the community.
  6. Archive and final report (by Dec 21, 2029): all documents are archived, and the final report goes to the sponsor. The project is formally closed on December 31, 2029.

After closing

Many preparedness projects fail after the ribbon-cutting. Equipment sits unused and skills fade. The federal mobile hospitals are an example: they cost about $7 million in one year just to store (Canadian Press, 2025). Sentinel Valley avoids this in three ways. The surge unit and stockpile are used every flu season. The digital twin produces a weekly respiratory forecast, so people use it in normal times. UFV keeps training graduates, so the skills stay in the valley. Because the network is used every week, it will be working when an outbreak happens.

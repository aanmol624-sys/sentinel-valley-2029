Sentinel Valley 2029 is an early-warning network for the next pandemic.

A $1 billion proposal for Chacha & the University of the Fraser Valley: Canada's first One Health early-warning and response network, designed to help the Fraser Valley avoid another shutdown like 2020.

When I started this proposal, I asked myself a "time machine" question: if I could go back to December 2019 with what we know and the technology we have today, what would I build in the Fraser Valley? One hospital or one app would not have been enough. COVID-19 showed that several things fail together. Warnings arrive late, people spread the virus before they feel sick, indoor air carries it, supplies run out and, in the end, whole communities shut down (Independent Panel for Pandemic Preparedness and Response, 2021).

The Fraser Valley also has a risk that most of Canada does not. About 80% of British Columbia's poultry farms are here (CTV News, 2022), and avian influenza has hit them in waves since 2022, with about 8.7 million birds culled in B.C. (CTV News, 2025). In November 2024, Canada's first locally acquired human case of H5N1 was a B.C. teenager. Fraser Health led the investigation, and the virus most closely matched wild birds found in the Fraser Valley (B.C. Ministry of Health, 2024a, 2024b). The next pandemic is a "Disease X" that nobody can name yet (World Health Organization [WHO], 2024b). But if it jumps from animals to people in Canada, our valley is one of the most likely places for that to happen.

Because the valley is a likely spillover point, Sentinel Valley would make it an early-warning site for Canada, with five connected capabilities that I call the 5S:

Project charter

Course topics on this page: 16. Project charter 7. Champion

The charter formally authorizes the project and names who is responsible for it (Renso, 2026). In my last assignment I lost a mark on the charter, so this one includes every field.

Project titleSentinel Valley 2029: One Health Pandemic Early-Warning and Response Network
SponsorChacha & the University of the Fraser Valley (recipient of the $1,000,000,000 investment)
ChampionUFV President and Vice-Chancellor (see below)
Project managerAnmol, Sentinel Valley Project Management Office (PMO), UFV Abbotsford
Start / go-live / closeJanuary 4, 2027 / October 1, 2029 / December 31, 2029
Budget$1,000,000,000, including a $100M contingency reserve and a $68.6M operating reserve for 2030–2034 (Cost page)
ProblemThe Fraser Valley is a likely place for animal-to-human spillover, depends on outside supply routes that floods have cut, and shut down in 2020 because it was not prepared for a pandemic.
Objectives (SMART)
  1. Operate 25 wastewater sentinel sites, 400 farm sentinel sites and 40 primary-care sentinel clinics, reporting signals to public-health officials within 48 hours, by June 30, 2029.
  2. Deliver a validated Fraser Valley Digital Twin that can simulate an outbreak and 5 response options within 24 hours, by June 29, 2029.
  3. Upgrade air in 103 public schools, 45 care facilities, 160 farm-worker and processing sites, 4 UFV campuses and 30 community hubs to at least 5 equivalent air changes per hour, by August 30, 2029.
  4. Open a flood-proof regional depot stocked with 10 million PPE items and a 100-bed surge unit, by July 31, 2029.
  5. Train 900 UFV students and 2,000 volunteers, and complete 1,100 continuity plans, by December 14, 2029.
Success criteriaAll five objectives accepted through scope validation. Spending within budget (CPI ≥ 0.95). The full-scale exercise "Operation Sentinel" (Sept 2029) turns a simulated signal into an official alert within 72 hours.
ApproachA program of five coordinated projects. Construction follows a Waterfall life cycle; the digital twin and app follow a Spiral life cycle. Phase exits and kill points sit at each milestone.
Key assumptionsPartners (Fraser Health, BCCDC, CFIA, municipalities, school districts) sign agreements by April 2027. No health data is used before privacy impact assessments are approved.
ConstraintsFixed budget. A hard close date of Dec 31, 2029. School work only in summer. UFV has no legal authority over health orders.
RolesPillar leads: SENSE (UFV Agriculture Centre of Excellence), SIMULATE (UFV Computing Information Systems), SHIELD (facilities engineering lead), SUPPLY (logistics lead), SUSTAIN (UFV Health Sciences). A steering committee meets monthly.
Sign-offSponsor, champion, project manager and Fraser Health liaison (signatures required at M1, Feb 26, 2027)
CommentsFraser Health: data access requires a signed information-sharing agreement. Finance: the contingency can only be released by the steering committee.

7Who is the champion?

A champion selects and protects a project, gets resources and approvals on time, and wins cooperation from other departments (Renso, 2026). My champion is the President and Vice-Chancellor of UFV. The President can work with the university, the cities of Abbotsford and Chilliwack, and Fraser Health. The President is also the public face of UFV in the region.

The champion's specific jobs are: chairing the steering committee, unblocking partner agreements in the first 120 days, defending the contingency reserve from being spent early, and publicly launching the network at go-live. A second, technical champion, the director of the UFV Agriculture Centre of Excellence in Chilliwack, gives credibility with farmers. In 2014, the centre had a barn with poultry and livestock areas (University of the Fraser Valley, 2014).

Scope

Course topics on this page: 2. Scope 8. Deliverables

Scope is the work that must be done (Renso, 2026). On a project this size, the biggest danger is scope creep: "while we're at it, let's also build…". This page lists what is in and out of scope.

2Scope management plan

Scope work has six parts: plan scope management, collect requirements, define scope, create the WBS, validate scope and control scope (Renso, 2026). Requirements come from three workshops in the first 90 days with Fraser Health, farm associations and school districts. Every requirement gets an ID and an owner, and is traced to a deliverable. Skipping this step leads to expensive rework (Renso, 2026).

In scope

  • Surveillance of wastewater, farms, wild birds, primary-care clinics and border points (population-level only)
  • The Fraser Valley Digital Twin, data centre, alert app and dashboard
  • Air upgrades in the buildings listed in the charter
  • A flood-proof depot, stockpile, rotation contracts, surge unit and mobile fleet
  • Training, exercises, continuity plans, community liaisons, and the 2030–2034 operating reserve

Out of scope

  • Building a new hospital or vaccine factory (too slow and outside UFV's role)
  • Issuing health orders, quarantines or border closures (see Charter)
  • Tracking people's phones or locations; all surveillance is population-level
  • Air upgrades for private homes and private businesses (except continuity-plan grants)

Work breakdown structure

The WBS breaks the work into levels (Renso, 2026). Level 1 is the project. Level 2 follows the process groups and the five pillars, and the same numbers appear on the Gantt chart:

  1. Initiating (charter, stakeholders)
  2. Planning (requirements, privacy, design)
  3. Procurement (RFPs, RFQs, contracts)
  4. SENSE (wastewater, farms, lab, global partnership)
  5. SIMULATE (data centre, digital twin, app, ISO 27001)
  6. SHIELD (assessments, schools, care homes, farm housing)
  7. SUPPLY (land, depot, stockpile, surge unit, fleet)
  8. SUSTAIN (training, continuity, exercises)
  9. Testing & acceptance
  10. Monitoring & controlling
  11. Closing

Validating and controlling scope

Scope validation is the formal acceptance of finished deliverables (Renso, 2026). Each deliverable above has a named person who signs it off, using a checklist written during planning, not afterwards. Scope control goes through integrated change control. Any request is logged, its effect on time, cost, quality and risk is analyzed, and the steering committee approves or rejects it. Seventy percent of projects spend at least 10% of their time managing changes (Renso, 2026), so I have budgeted time for change requests.

Why the Fraser Valley needs this before the next pandemic

The professor's question was how to avoid another catastrophic shutdown.

What the 2020 shutdown cost

On March 17, 2020, B.C. suspended K-12 classes indefinitely, and most of the province's 550,000 students were sent home (CBC News, 2020a). Between February and June 2020, B.C. lost 235,100 jobs (RBC Economics, 2020), and the province forecast a record $12.5 billion deficit (CBC News, 2020c). Federally, the Canada Emergency Response Benefit alone was projected to cost $71.3 billion (CBC News, 2020b). B.C.'s real GDP fell 3.1% in 2020 (BC Stats, 2024).

To put the $1 billion in perspective: it is about 1.4% of what CERB alone was projected to cost (CBC News, 2020b). If Sentinel Valley helps the region avoid even part of one future shutdown, the money has done its job. (See the ROI analysis on the Cost page.)

Why this valley is different

It is where animal viruses meet people

The Fraser Valley holds about 80% of B.C.'s poultry farms (CTV News, 2022). Since 2022, avian flu has forced repeated culls (CTV News, 2025). Spillover to people has already happened here. Fraser Valley poultry workers caught H7N3 in 2004 (CBC News, 2024), and in 2024 a B.C. teenager became Canada's first domestic H5N1 case, with a virus matching local wild birds (B.C. Ministry of Health, 2024b). WHO now asks countries to prepare for whole families of pathogens (WHO, 2024a). That is what a One Health design does, because it monitors animals and people together.

COVID hit the people who feed us

During COVID-19, Fraser Health focused on outbreaks in food processing, on farms and in nurseries "where we have workers in crowded accommodations, including our temporary farm workers" (Fraser Health, 2021). UFV researchers have already studied this problem with federal research funding (Jardine, 2021). Sentinel Valley builds on that local knowledge.

The valley can be cut off

In November 2021, floods and mudslides blocked every major highway connecting the Lower Mainland to the rest of Canada, and grocery shelves emptied (CBC News, 2021). The Fraser Valley East and Abbotsford regions produce over a third of B.C.'s farm revenue (Statistics Canada, 2021). A pandemic stockpile stored in a flood zone, or far away behind closed highways, would fail when we need it. Our supply depot will be local and on high ground.

What the evidence says works

  • Wastewater surveillance gives early, population-level signals and is cost-effective compared with mass swab testing (Sanjak et al., 2024). B.C. already tests wastewater for several respiratory viruses (BC Centre for Disease Control [BCCDC], n.d.), so we will expand that system.
  • Clean indoor air reduces exposure. The CDC recommends aiming for at least five air changes per hour (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention [CDC], n.d.). Far-UVC light reduced infectious airborne virus by more than 98% in an occupied room (Buonanno et al., 2024).
  • Digital twins of cities can simulate how a virus spreads and test interventions before officials use them (Bilal et al., 2025).
Sentinel Valley will not stop every infection or predict an unknown virus with certainty, and only provincial and medical health officers can issue health orders. It gives those officials earlier data, cleaner buildings, supplies and trained people.

Topic24

Handling stakeholders

Stakeholder management has four processes: identify, plan, manage engagement and control engagement (Renso, 2026).

  1. Identify
  2. Plan
  3. Manage engagement
  4. Control engagement
The hardest group

Poultry farmers

If they believe a positive sample means their whole flock is culled, they will refuse sampling. When avian flu is detected on a farm, CFIA requires all birds there to be euthanized. My plan gives farms biosecurity grants of about $110,000 each, which reduce the chance of infection in the first place, and it lets the B.C. Poultry Association help design the program.

Leading partner

Stó:lō communities

Stó:lō communities decide how data about their members is used. Liaisons are hired with Stó:lō guidance and a community seat sits on the steering committee.

Control engagement

Re-assessed every quarter

Engagement levels are re-assessed every quarter. If a key stakeholder slips back, for example from supportive to neutral, the champion meets them within two weeks.

Topic21

Communicating to stakeholders

The communication plan states who needs what information, when, in what format, from whom, and how issues are escalated (Renso, 2026). It uses all three methods from the lectures: interactive (two-way), push (sent out) and pull (people look it up).

Interactive

Two-way: meetings, briefings, stand-ups, working groups and town halls.

Push

Sent out: reports, emails, text messages and news releases.

Pull

People look it up: the website, the public dashboard and FAQs.

Escalation path

  1. Pillar lead · 5 working days
  2. PM · 5 more days
  3. Champion

Escalation: an issue that a pillar lead cannot solve in 5 working days goes to the PM. If the PM cannot solve it in 5 more days, it goes to the champion. Status reports describe where we stand on a given date, while progress reports describe what we accomplished, and the project uses both (Renso, 2026).

Topic12

Progress reports

Every pillar lead sends a weekly update in the format taught in class: work completed, working on, going well, not going well, suggestions and issues, and project updates (Renso, 2026). Example: the SHIELD weekly update.

Without regular updates, problems stay hidden until they become crises (Renso, 2026). Monthly reports also feed the earned-value numbers on the Cost page.

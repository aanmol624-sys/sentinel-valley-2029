Topic3

Cost management

Planning, estimating, budgeting and controlling.

Cost management covers planning, estimating, budgeting and controlling (Renso, 2026). This proposal is a rough order of magnitude (ROM) estimate. It answers "what will this cost?" before the design exists. Estimates at this stage can be off by −30% to +50% (Alberta Infrastructure, 2025), which is why I hold a 10% contingency. The definitive estimate comes after detailed design (milestone M2), using RFQ prices from real vendors.

Direct costs include the lab, HVAC equipment and PPE. Indirect costs include the PMO, electricity, and insurance inside the building lines. Intangible benefits, such as trust and UFV's reputation (Renso, 2026), are not given a dollar value. Construction-type items use benchmarks that already exist: B.C. and Ottawa spent $4.2 million on ventilation in 8 schools (Infrastructure Canada, 2024), about $525,000 per school, and that is my unit cost.

Benchmark unit cost$525,000

per school ventilation upgrade: $4.2M for 8 B.C. schools (Infrastructure Canada, 2024)

Learning from Ottawa's mistakes

I also learned from Ottawa's mistakes. It paid more than $200 million for four mobile hospitals that mostly sat in storage (Canadian Press, 2025), and it discarded expired PPE in 2018–2019 (Health Canada, 2021). Our surge unit will be used every flu season, and stock will rotate through Fraser Health before it expires. We also set aside 6.9% of the budget to keep things running after 2029.

Controlling cost with earned value

Earned value compares spending with the work completed (Renso, 2026). The PMO reports earned value monthly. Example for the end of Q2 2028:

$420MPlanned value (PV)
$395MEarned value (EV)
$410MActual cost (AC)
Cost variance−$15M

CV = EV − AC = −$15M (over budget for the work done)

Schedule variance−$25M

SV = EV − PV = −$25M (behind schedule)

Cost performance index0.96

CPI = EV ÷ AC = 0.96

Under my rule, a CPI below 0.95 or an SV worse than −5% of PV triggers a recovery plan. In this example the CPI is still acceptable, but the schedule needs attention.
Topic14

Return on investment and payback

Three scenarios, 2027–2049, discounted at 3%.

ROI compares total discounted benefits with total discounted costs, and payback is the time it takes to recover the investment (Renso, 2026). My model and its assumptions are below. B.C.'s real GDP in 2019 was about $278 billion (derived from BC Stats, 2024). In 2020 it fell 3.1% instead of growing about 2.9%, a gap of roughly 6%. The Fraser Valley is about 6.2% of B.C.'s population (Statistics Canada, 2022; Pacific Economic Development Canada, 2022). If the valley's share of the loss matched its share of the population, one pandemic year cost the valley an estimated $1.04 billion.

In conclusion, Sentinel Valley pays for itself only if a serious pandemic happens and the network works well. The model leaves out lives saved and avoided government emergency spending, because these are hard to measure. For scale, CERB alone was projected at $71.3 billion (CBC News, 2020b). This is preparedness spending, like insurance. Its value shows up in the years when something goes wrong, which is why the business case is based on protecting the region from losses.

Organization

Course topics on this page: 5. Three spheres of system management 6. Four frames of organizations (UFV) 9. Life cycle model (Waterfall / Spiral) 10. Initiating, planning, executing, monitoring & controlling, closing 11. Pre-initiation tasks

Every project depends on the organization that carries it (Renso, 2026). Here I analyze Sentinel Valley as a system, and UFV as the organization that has to carry it.

5Three spheres of system management

Business view

A $1B network for about 324,000 people makes sense if it protects a regional economy whose one-year pandemic loss I estimate at about $1 billion. It also protects the poultry industry, and the network keeps producing value between pandemics through flu-season data and training.

Organization view

Fraser Health must accept data from a university, farmers must allow sampling on their land, and school districts must schedule summer construction. The technology depends on these agreements.

Technology view

Sensors, lab equipment, a secure data platform, a digital twin and an alert app all have to connect. Most of the technology, like wastewater testing and HVAC, is already proven. Only the digital twin is new, and it is built in spiral cycles.

Systems thinking means looking at a project as a whole, inside the organization where it has to work, not as a set of separate parts (Renso, 2026). A network that works technically can still fail if the business case is weak or the partners do not support it. That is why every pillar has an owner in each sphere: a business lead who tracks value for money, an organization lead who manages partner agreements, and a technical lead who makes the systems connect. When a change request arrives, I ask three questions. Does it still make business sense? Will the partners accept it? Will the technology still fit together?

6Four frames of organizations: UFV

Bolman and Deal (2017) describe four frames for understanding any organization. The lectures ask us to apply them to UFV (Renso, 2026).

Structural frame

UFV is organized into faculties, campuses and administrative units. Sentinel Valley needs people from Agriculture, Computing Information Systems, Health Sciences and Facilities to work together. I propose a matrix structure: staff stay in their faculty but report to a pillar lead for project work. The PMO sits under the Provost, and the steering committee holds the decision rights.

Human resource frame

If the project simply adds work, people will resist. Because faculty and staff already have full workloads, the budget pays for course releases, new hires and co-op students (see People), and gives faculty research opportunities they value.

Political frame

Resources are scarce, so campuses may compete for the labs and municipalities will want credit. Abbotsford gets the data centre and Chilliwack gets the genomics lab, close to the farms. Farm groups may fear that surveillance leads to culls. The champion's job is to negotiate these interests openly, and stakeholder agreements make the trade-offs explicit.

Symbolic frame

UFV's identity is a university that serves the Fraser Valley and works on Stó:lō territory. The name "Sentinel Valley" presents the valley as Canada's early-warning site, and launch events and the dashboard use that message.

9Project life cycle: Waterfall and Spiral

I use both models, because the deliverables are different. Buildings, HVAC and the depot follow Waterfall, because construction cannot be done in cycles. That work moves through analysis, design, construction, testing and support in order (Renso, 2026). The digital twin, alert app and dashboard follow Spiral. The lecture describes Spiral as non-linear, revisiting all phases. Each cycle plans, builds, tests and evaluates a version with real public-health users, then the next cycle improves it. Four cycles run from January 2028 to June 2029. The whole project also moves through the concept, development, implementation and close-out phases, with phase exits and kill points at each milestone (Renso, 2026).

Running two models side by side needs one rule: the spiral software must be ready to plug into the buildings when they open. Each spiral cycle ends with a demonstration to public-health users, and cycle 4 must finish before integrated testing starts in June 2029. If a cycle runs late, I cut features from the next cycle instead of moving the deadline, which protects the critical path on the Time page.

10The five process groups in this project

Process groupWhat happens in Sentinel ValleyWhen
InitiatingCharter signed, champion and sponsor confirmed, stakeholder register, partner MOUsJan–Apr 2027
PlanningRequirements, WBS, schedule, budget, privacy assessments, risk, quality, communication and procurement plansMar–Sep 2027
ExecutingBuilding the five pillars, hiring, training, contractingJun 2027–Sep 2029
Monitoring & controllingMonthly progress reports, earned value, risk register reviews, change control. This runs during all the other groupsJan 2027–Dec 2029
ClosingAcceptance, contract close-out, lessons learned, handover to operationsOct–Dec 2029

The process groups overlap; they do not happen one after another. Executing means coordinating people and resources to carry out the plans, while monitoring and controlling means measuring progress and taking corrective action (Renso, 2026). In Sentinel Valley, planning continues during execution because each spiral cycle of the digital twin is planned just before it starts. If the earned-value numbers on the Cost page slip, the project goes back to planning and adjusts the baseline through change control. Each group also ends with a clear output: a signed charter, an approved baseline, accepted deliverables and a final report.

11Pre-initiation tasks

Six tasks come before the project officially starts (Renso, 2026). Each task is covered as follows:

  1. Determine scope, time and cost: Done at ROM level in this proposal.
  2. Identify the sponsor: Chacha & UFV.
  3. Select a project manager: I lead the PMO, supported by an experienced, PMP-certified deputy.
  4. Develop a business case: The Why page and the ROI analysis.
  5. Hold meetings to agree on process and expectations: A kickoff with Fraser Health, BCCDC, CFIA and the school districts in December 2026.
  6. Determine size and split into mini-projects: The 5S pillars are five manageable projects inside one program.

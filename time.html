Time: schedule and Gantt chart

Course topics on this page: 1. Time

The work runs from January 4, 2027 to a formal close on December 31, 2029. The system goes live on October 1, 2029, which leaves three months for closing.

How the schedule was built

Scheduling followed the seven steps from the lectures: plan schedule management, define activities, sequence them, estimate resources, estimate durations, develop the schedule and control it (Renso, 2026).

Dependencies

  • Mandatory (hard logic): the depot must be built (7.2) before the stockpile can move in (7.3). Integrated testing (9.1) cannot start until the systems exist.
  • Discretionary: I chose to finish privacy impact assessments (2.2) before any data work on the digital twin begins, even though design could technically overlap.
  • External: school HVAC work can only happen in July and August, when students are away. So SHIELD work is split into two summer waves (6.2a and 6.2b).

The critical path

The longest chain of dependent work is: charter, then requirements, then detailed design, then baseline approval, then procurement, then depot land, depot construction, stockpile, integrated testing, Operation Sentinel, go-live and closing. Any delay on this chain delays the whole project. The PMO tracks it weekly, and the depot contract has late-delivery penalties. Click "Critical path" above to see it.

SMART milestones

Milestones on a Gantt chart should be specific, measurable, assignable, realistic and time-framed (Renso, 2026):

MilestoneDateMeasured byOwner
M1 Charter approvedFeb 26, 2027Four signatures on the charterChampion

M3 Major contracts awardedNov 30, 2027Signed contracts for depot, lab, HVAC, computeProcurement lead

M5 Project closedDec 31, 2029All deliverables accepted, contracts closed, lessons learned filedPM

PERT estimate and project buffer

Estimates are uncertain, so a project buffer should always exist (Renso, 2026). For the riskiest task, depot construction, I used PERT: optimistic 10 months, most likely 13 months, pessimistic 19 months. The PERT estimate is (10 + 4×13 + 19) ÷ 6 = 13.5 months, which is the duration planned on the chart. Go-live is three months before the hard close date, and that gap is the project buffer.

Controlling the schedule

If a team misses a deadline, I act within five working days (Renso, 2026). A slipped milestone triggers a recovery plan: re-sequencing, adding temporary staff, or using contingency funds. If two milestones slip in a row, the issue goes to the steering committee.
